# ADR 0004 — Daily activity contributes to training pressure

The training ring includes ordinary walking as well as exercise. Its 0–21 number is a product estimate, not a clinical stress measurement. All calculations stay in Postgres; the app displays the published result.

`nb.training_load_ticks` is the single contribution stream for the total, cumulative curve, zone time, source breakdown and evidence; `nb.activity_ticks` is its compatibility projection. Each completed five-minute origin slot contributes the greater of heart-rate-zone load and movement load. Taking the greater signal avoids counting the same activity twice. Missing personal resting heart rate disables heart-rate zones, but does not discard recorded steps or MET. Missing observations stay missing; an observed zero step count can produce zero movement without claiming recording coverage or drawing a continuous curve through empty placeholders.

Live sport heart-rate callbacks are committed to the account's durable outbox and uploaded as immutable, timestamped evidence. Only adjacent reports in the same session and continuity, at most 15 seconds apart, define observed intervals. A report holds until the next report; the last report is never extended. Pauses, disconnects, stream changes and failed local commits break continuity. Overlapping intervals count once. Within each five-minute bucket fine intervals replace the corresponding coarse heart-rate duration, while movement is still compared once. A single isolated report may supply a peak but no duration. Zone minutes are floored separately; precise seconds remain in the published evidence. Historical SDK sport records need a verified timestamp and pause mapping before ingestion.

Exercise resting heart rate requires at least three qualified, independent nights. It uses the prior ISO week's median, or the preceding seven days when that week is insufficient; the latter is labelled estimated. Qualification reuses the shared recovery evidence gate: at least 60 covered minutes, at least half of located sleep minutes, and no gap over 90 minutes. These are engineering quality gates, not clinical validation. Duplicate source nights do not add baseline observations. The suggested target remains the existing nine-band rule based on final wake reserve, with data quality exposed; training goals such as BULK do not yet produce a validated individual training dose.

Published `daily_training.recorded_steps` sums every eligible origin slot independently of activity segmentation. Evidence separates elapsed, recorded, heart-rate and movement time, and exposes baseline nights and provenance. Recording coverage does not establish wear time. Training algorithm revision `tl-2.2` invalidates prior derived results through the existing ordered replay pipeline; source facts remain intact.

Movement uses the recorded MET when present. Otherwise steps provide an explicitly approximate walking intensity: 500 steps in five minutes corresponds to 3 MET, with the estimate capped at 5 MET. The fallback assumes walking cadence and is not a vendor measurement. Movement contributes `0.375 × max(MET − 1, 0)` raw points per five minutes. This matches the existing zone-1 contribution at 3 MET and gives stronger heart-rate exercise zones greater emphasis. These product coefficients need future calibration against observed activity; they are not claimed to be clinically validated.

Daytime HRV does not multiply accumulated physical work. HRV depends on acquisition conditions and movement artifacts; the existing night-HRV → night inputs → Body Battery → suggested target path is retained. Reference: [HRV standardisation checklist](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7082649/). MET definition and activity context: [2024 Compendium of Physical Activities](https://pacompendium.com/).

Energy uses net MET above resting, body weight and recorded duration. Vendor calories are not added to calculated basal energy because their basal component is not separable. The UI identifies movement calories as estimates. Missing movement observations or required body measurements remain null. Basal and active components are rounded separately, and their exact sum is the total displayed.

The resting baseline uses Mifflin–St Jeor with the effective age, sex, height and weight. A body scan's `bmr_kcal` is retained and labelled as a device estimate; it is not a direct resting-metabolism measurement and does not silently replace the baseline. [The original Mifflin study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2305711/) validated a population prediction against indirect calorimetry; it does not establish an individual's true expenditure. A future measured resting-metabolism override needs its source, measurement date and quality recorded separately. Neither averaging two predictions nor adding more correlated device outputs establishes accuracy.

The September 2026 energy audit found that an older deployed `compute_fuel` body did not match a string-replacement migration, leaving its total on the legacy formula. The corrective migration replaces complete function definitions and calculates OUT and balance from `fuel_components`. The five-minute energy numerator is summed before dividing by 240 (`net MET × kg × 21 / 240`), avoiding decimal approximation of 1/12 at half-kcal rounding boundaries. Historical publications are invalidated for normal replay; raw measurements are preserved.

Recorded MET must survive upload, archive merging and paginated client reads. The UI uses the same bounded MET preference and step fallback for attribution as the backend. Baseline and movement are distributed separately; unknown movement cannot be replaced with zero or inserted into the final hour to manufacture a peak. These are estimates for the observed intervals, not proof of complete wear or a calibrated total daily energy expenditure.

A day prefers the latest weight before local 04:00. If none exists, the first weight recorded during that day becomes available once received. Future measurements never fill earlier days. All calculations use the effective profile and calculation instant, preserving replay behavior. Existing published days are invalidated when the migration installs so the next sync recomputes them.

Resting energy is prorated over the actual 04:00-to-04:00 user-day duration, as specified in F2. This preserves the full daily baseline at closure on 23/25-hour daylight-saving days. Coverage counts unique five-minute slots against that actual window. Correcting an erroneous weight remains a factual edit; a later different weigh-in does not prove the earlier entry was wrong.
