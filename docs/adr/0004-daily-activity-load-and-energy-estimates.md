# ADR 0004 — Daily activity contributes to training pressure

The training ring includes ordinary walking as well as exercise. Its 0–21 number is a product estimate, not a clinical stress measurement. All calculations stay in Postgres; the app displays the published result.

`nb.training_ledger` owns observed intervals; `nb.training_load_ticks` aggregates them for the total, cumulative curve, zone time and evidence, and `nb.activity_ticks` is its compatibility projection. Each completed five-minute origin slot is split at fine observation boundaries; overlapping heart-rate and movement signals contribute their greater load for the same seconds. Taking the greater signal avoids counting the same activity twice, without letting a later cardio interval erase earlier strength work in the same slot. Before a qualified personal resting baseline exists, heart rate uses explicitly estimated age-maximum zones; missing age still prevents this estimate. Missing observations stay missing; an observed zero step count can produce zero movement without claiming recording coverage or drawing a continuous curve through empty placeholders.

Live sport heart-rate callbacks are committed to the account's durable outbox and uploaded as immutable, timestamped evidence. Only adjacent reports in the same session and continuity, at most 15 seconds apart, define observed intervals. A report holds until the next report; the last report is never extended. Pauses, disconnects, stream changes and failed local commits break continuity. Overlapping intervals count once. Within each five-minute bucket fine intervals replace the corresponding coarse heart-rate duration, while movement is still compared once. A single isolated report may supply a peak but no duration. Zone minutes are floored separately; precise seconds remain in the published evidence. Historical SDK sport records need a verified timestamp and pause mapping before ingestion.

Exercise resting heart rate requires at least three qualified, independent nights. It uses the prior ISO week's median, or the preceding seven days when that week is insufficient; the latter is labelled estimated. Qualification reuses the shared recovery evidence gate: at least 60 covered minutes, at least half of located sleep minutes, and no gap over 90 minutes. These are engineering quality gates, not clinical validation. Duplicate source nights do not add baseline observations. Without that baseline, age-maximum zones start at 50/60/70/80/90 percent and are identified as `age_max_estimate`; they never invent a resting-HR value. Training goals such as BULK do not by themselves establish a validated individual training dose.

Published `daily_training.recorded_steps` sums every eligible origin slot independently of activity segmentation. Evidence separates elapsed, recorded, heart-rate and movement time, and exposes baseline nights and provenance. Recording coverage does not establish wear time. Training algorithm revision `tl-3.0` invalidates prior derived results through the existing ordered replay pipeline; source facts remain intact.

## Sleep-led targets and recorded session contributions · September 15, 2026

The nine wake-reserve bands are now an older-server client fallback. New publications carry `daily_training.evidence.target`; a present null target is authoritative. Sleep scores are refreshed before training publication in the same settlement. A completed canonical night is required. Sleep readiness combines duration/recovery/architecture/regularity scores at 40/40/10/10, normalizing only present components. That sleep readiness supplies 80 percent of the recommendation input and wake reserve 20 percent, again normalizing missing components. The continuous base is `4 + 12 × readiness / 100`, bounded to 4–16. Nights below six hours cap it continuously from 8 at four hours or less to 11 just before six hours. The normal suggested band is target ±2, within the 0–21 scale.

Recent load can reduce the base by at most two points: 0.35 times the positive difference between the preceding three-day mean and the prior 25-day median. This requires two eligible recent days and seven earlier days. Eligibility requires this algorithm revision, at least six recorded hours and at least half of the day's elapsed minutes; absent or incomplete recordings never count as rest days. Today's load is excluded from this baseline. `limited` identifies missing recovery physiology, an incomplete personal sleep baseline or absent wake reserve. These weights and bounds are product calibration, not a clinical or individually validated exercise prescription. Sleep loss is a relevant input, but the [sleep-loss performance research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35708888/) does not validate a mapping to this app's 0–21 scale.

Body Battery composes this sleep-led base with its observed reserve limit (`target-1.1`). The published target is the lower of the two when that limit exists. With no reserve limit, the completed-night base remains available. An older observation retains its fatigue restriction and original timestamp, with `reserve_fresh: false`; becoming stale cannot itself raise the target. Both the base and the reserve adjustment travel with the result; the app and AI read the same final target. The scale maximum 21 is never presented as a training quota or as the amount still recommended.

Explicit live sport intervals earn their full intensity from the first observed interval. They do not wait for a 15-minute high-HR run. Unlabelled daily activity retains the earlier sustained-run rule and quarter weight outside qualifying runs. Explicit strength running-state receipts supply their existing conservative MET estimate even without optical contact; the same seconds replace coarse movement evidence. Fine HR and strength receipts from a new client share one session ID. Overlapping receipts count once. A received endpoint within 15 seconds after a closed day's midnight can prove the preceding seconds, while integration remains clipped to each user day; an open day never uses future reports.

`evidence.sessions` publishes each actual session's observed seconds, raw contribution and contribution to the nonlinear daily curve. Contributions are differences in time order, not standalone workout scores or proportional shares that shrink when a later workout arrives. `displayed_delta` uses successive one-decimal cumulative differences so all displayed sources add to the ring; `load_delta` preserves sub-tenth changes. `segments` also contains `RECORDED SESSION` rows for older readers; new clients omit these duplicates when showing `evidence.sessions`. Unknown optical HR does not hide a strength session. Saturation and sub-tenth contributions are labelled explicitly.

Ending a session requests a fresh outbox drain after any in-flight publication. The recap remains pending until its session ID appears in the published day and updates from that publication, including after an offline retry. It does not snapshot an unchanged before/after pair before settlement or synthesize a production load locally.

Movement uses the recorded MET when present. Otherwise steps provide an explicitly approximate walking intensity: 500 steps in five minutes corresponds to 3 MET, with the estimate capped at 5 MET. The fallback assumes walking cadence and is not a vendor measurement. Movement contributes `0.375 × max(MET − 1, 0)` raw points per five minutes. This matches the existing zone-1 contribution at 3 MET and gives stronger heart-rate exercise zones greater emphasis. These product coefficients need future calibration against observed activity; they are not claimed to be clinically validated.

Daytime HRV does not multiply accumulated physical work. HRV depends on acquisition conditions and movement artifacts; eligible overnight recovery informs the sleep-led recommendation, while current Body Battery can limit it. Reference: [HRV standardisation checklist](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7082649/). MET definition and activity context: [2024 Compendium of Physical Activities](https://pacompendium.com/).

Energy uses net MET above resting, body weight and recorded duration. Vendor calories are not added to calculated basal energy because their basal component is not separable. The UI identifies movement calories as estimates. Missing movement observations or required body measurements remain null. Basal and active components are rounded separately, and their exact sum is the total displayed.

The September 2026 strength-energy correction routes explicit Weightlifting, Dumbbell, Squat training and Push-ups modes through conservative session-average MET values (3.5, 3.5, 5.0 and 3.0 respectively). Adjacent running reports in one continuity, no more than 15 seconds apart, establish the recorded duration independently of optical heart-rate contact. Pauses, gaps, clock reversal and ambiguous Fitness/Common modes break or exclude this evidence. The durable rows contain the mode and timestamps rather than client-computed calories, so server replay remains authoritative. Within a five-minute origin slot, strength energy replaces the same observed seconds of coarse movement energy; overlapping sessions own each second once. This is an explainable engineering estimate based on the Compendium, not individual calorimetry validation.

An available profile is not itself calorie evidence. The live UI publishes an estimated number only after at least one valid interval has been integrated; an authoritative native device report can publish an observed zero. Historical burn eligibility likewise counts only distinct slots with a usable MET or step-derived energy observation. Heart-rate-only rows do not establish all-day energy coverage. Coverage remains a gate for using historical days in a diet target and does not turn partial recordings into calibrated total daily expenditure. This correction is versioned as `energy-1.1`; existing derived days are invalidated for ordered replay while raw facts remain unchanged.

The resting baseline uses Mifflin–St Jeor with the effective age, sex, height and weight when the body has never been scanned. Since ADR 0025 (fuel-2.0) the latest body scan's `bmr_kcal` *is* the day's resting figure, published with its source (`day_fuel.resting_source`) and the scan's date rather than silently; it remains a device estimate, not a direct resting-metabolism measurement, and the product chose it because it is the number the wearer was shown. [The original Mifflin study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2305711/) validated a population prediction against indirect calorimetry; it does not establish an individual's true expenditure. A future measured resting-metabolism override needs its source, measurement date and quality recorded separately. Neither averaging two predictions nor adding more correlated device outputs establishes accuracy.

The September 2026 energy audit found that an older deployed `compute_fuel` body did not match a string-replacement migration, leaving its total on the legacy formula. The corrective migration replaces complete function definitions and calculates OUT and balance from `fuel_components`. The five-minute energy numerator is summed before dividing by 240 (`net MET × kg × 21 / 240`), avoiding decimal approximation of 1/12 at half-kcal rounding boundaries. Historical publications are invalidated for normal replay; raw measurements are preserved.

Recorded MET must survive upload, archive merging and paginated client reads. The UI uses the same bounded MET preference and step fallback for attribution as the backend. Baseline and movement are distributed separately; unknown movement cannot be replaced with zero or inserted into the final hour to manufacture a peak. These are estimates for the observed intervals, not proof of complete wear or a calibrated total daily energy expenditure.

As of #32 (2026-09-21), a day uses the latest weight at or before its calculation instant and before the next local day boundary. A manual body-profile save enters the next settlement; the clock includes a just-saved weight even inside the current minute. Future measurements never fill earlier days. All calculations use the effective profile and calculation instant, preserving replay behavior. Profile and weigh-in writes invalidate their existing calculation dependencies.

Resting energy is prorated over the actual user-day duration, as specified in F2 (midnight to midnight since ADR 0020). This preserves the full daily baseline at closure on 23/25-hour daylight-saving days. Coverage counts unique five-minute slots against that actual window. Correcting an erroneous weight remains a factual edit; a later different weigh-in does not prove the earlier entry was wrong.
